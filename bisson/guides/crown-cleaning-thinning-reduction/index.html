Crown Cleaning, Thinning and Reduction: What Each Pruning Term Means

Crown cleaning removes dead, dying and broken branches. Crown thinning selectively removes live branches to let in light and air and reduce wind load. Crown raising takes off low branches for clearance, and crown reduction shortens the height or spread of a tree. If you searched for crown clearing, you most likely mean crown cleaning or raising. Each one is a different job on the same tree, so knowing the name helps you ask for the right tree pruning work.

What the crown is and why it gets pruned

The crown is everything above the trunk: the main limbs, the branches and the leaves. Pruning the crown changes how much weight, wind and light the tree deals with, so the same word can mean very different amounts of cutting. The terms below are the ones arborists use, and a clear estimate should name which one applies to your tree.

Crown cleaning

Crown cleaning removes dead, dying, diseased and broken branches, and branches that rub against each other. It does not thin out healthy growth. It is the lightest of the four and the most common first step, because dead wood is the part most likely to drop in a storm or in a thaw. See our guide to warning signs in a tree for how to spot it from the ground.

Crown thinning

Crown thinning selectively removes some live branches, spread evenly through the crown, to reduce density. It lets light and air through and lowers the sail effect in wind. It does not make the tree smaller, and it is not meant to leave it bare inside. Thinning too hard can push the tree to grow weak shoots, so the amount removed matters more than the name.

Crown raising

Crown raising removes the lower branches to clear a walkway, a driveway, a roofline or a view. It is the job people often mean when they say crown clearing. Only the lowest limbs come off, and enough live crown has to stay on the upper tree so it is not left top-heavy.

Crown reduction

Crown reduction reduces the height or spread of a tree by cutting each branch back to a suitable side branch. It is used where a tree has outgrown its space or where a long limb is heavy over a roof. It is not topping. Cuts are made at a lateral branch that is large enough to take over, and the tree keeps its natural shape. Reduction is a bigger cut than cleaning or thinning, and some species tolerate it better than others.

What not to ask for: topping

Topping chops branches back to stubs with no regard for the branch structure. It leaves open wounds, triggers a flush of weak, fast growth, and shortens the life of the tree. If an estimate only says "cut it back", ask which of the four cuts above is meant.

How to choose

  • Dead branches over the house or the yard: crown cleaning.
  • A dense tree that catches a lot of wind, or shades a lawn heavily: crown thinning.
  • Branches in the way of a driveway, a path or a roofline: crown raising.
  • A tree that has simply gotten too big for the spot: crown reduction, or removal if reduction would ruin it.
  • A tree with a split, a lean or hollow wood: see an arborist first. It may need support or removal rather than pruning.

When to do it

Dead wood can come off at any time of year. For live pruning, timing matters for some species, as covered in our guide to when to prune trees in Ottawa. The leaves are off in winter, which makes the structure easier to read.

Why not do it yourself

Crown work is done high in the tree, with a saw, often over a house or a wire. Our guide to pruning large branches covers what is realistic from the ground. Anything above a ladder's reach, or near a wire, is crew work.

Permits and City trees

Pruning that maintains a tree and follows good practice does not normally need a permit under Ottawa's Tree Protection By-law, but heavy cuts or damage can still count as injuring a protected tree. See the permit guide if removal is on the table. A tree on the City boulevard belongs to the City: call 3-1-1.

Common questions

What is the difference between crown cleaning and crown thinning?

Crown cleaning removes only dead, dying, diseased or broken branches. Crown thinning removes some healthy live branches as well, selectively, to reduce density and let light and air through.

What is crown clearing?

It is a common name for work around the crown. Most people mean crown cleaning (removing dead wood) or crown raising (removing low branches for clearance). Ask the arborist to describe exactly which branches will come off.

How much of a tree's crown can be removed in one visit?

As little as the goal needs. Heavy removal stresses a tree. A good arborist limits how much live crown comes off at once and explains why.

Is crown reduction the same as topping?

No. Reduction cuts back to suitable side branches and keeps the natural shape. Topping chops branches to stubs and leaves weak regrowth and open wounds.

Do I need a permit for pruning in Ottawa?

Pruning that maintains a tree and follows good practice does not normally need a permit under Ottawa's Tree Protection By-law, but heavy cuts or damage can still count as injuring a protected tree. A City boulevard tree belongs to the City, so call 3-1-1.
