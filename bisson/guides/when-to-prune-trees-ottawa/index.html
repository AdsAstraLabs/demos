When to Prune Trees in Ottawa

For most trees in Ottawa the best time to prune is late winter, while the tree is dormant: you can see the structure, disease pressure is low, and cuts start closing as soon as growth begins in spring. The exceptions matter: do not prune oaks from April to July, prune spring-flowering trees right after they bloom, and remove dead or broken branches whenever you find them. In Ottawa, pruning done to good arboricultural practice needs no City permit.

Late winter: the main window

From about February into early spring, most deciduous trees are dormant. With the leaves gone, it is easy to see crossing branches, weak forks and deadwood. Insects and fungi are inactive, so fresh cuts are less likely to be infected, and the tree starts sealing them within weeks once it wakes up. Frozen ground also means less damage to your lawn and beds.

The exceptions

Some trees need different timing:

  • Oak: prune only in the dormant season, never April to July. Oak wilt, a fatal disease spread by beetles drawn to fresh wounds, has been confirmed in Ontario.
  • Maple and birch: they bleed sap heavily when cut in late winter. It looks alarming but is harmless to a healthy tree; if you would rather not see it, wait until the leaves are fully out.
  • Dead, broken or hanging branches: any time of year. Season does not matter once a branch is a hazard.

When to avoid pruning

The least good times are when leaves are coming out in spring, when the tree has spent its reserves and bark tears easily, and during leaf drop in autumn, when fungi are spreading spores. Avoid heavy pruning during a drought, too: a stressed tree copes badly with losing leaf area.

How much to take

Less than people expect. A good rule is to remove no more than about a quarter of the living crown in a year, cut back to a branch or the trunk collar rather than leaving stubs, and never top a tree: topping causes weak, fast regrowth and decay that shortens the tree's life. Deadwood, crossing branches and weak forks come first.

Permits and City trees

Under Ottawa's Tree Protection By-law, pruning that maintains a tree's health and follows good arboricultural practice needs no permit. Heavy cuts, topping or root damage can still count as injuring a protected tree. A tree on the City boulevard belongs to the City: report problems to 3-1-1 rather than pruning it yourself.

Common questions

What is the best month to prune trees in Ottawa?

Late winter, roughly February to March, for most deciduous trees. Oaks only in the dormant season, and spring-flowering trees right after they bloom.

Do I need a permit to prune a tree in Ottawa?

No, not for pruning that maintains the tree's health and follows good arboricultural practice. Heavy cuts or topping can still count as injuring a protected tree.

Why shouldn't oaks be pruned in summer?

Fresh wounds attract the beetles that spread oak wilt, a fatal disease confirmed in Ontario. Prune oaks only while dormant, never April to July.
